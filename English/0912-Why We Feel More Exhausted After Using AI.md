# Why We Feel More Exhausted After Using AI

::: tip 适用说明
- **适用场景**：日常闲聊 / 吐槽工作 / 口语跟读模仿
- **语速参考**：120–140 词/分钟（美式日常松弛语速）
- **学习路径**：先记高频词与小词链，再学短语与场景，最后整段跟读模仿语流
:::

## 板块一：修正后的口语主题标题

**英文标题**：Why We Feel More Exhausted After Using AI

原句 *how come we even more exhausted after using AI compared with work without AI* 有几个典型的中国学习者问题：

1. **how come** 后面必须跟完整句子（"how come **we're** even more exhausted"），漏掉 be 动词是常见错误；
2. **compared with work without AI** 太长太绕——口语里直接说 "than when we worked without it"；
3. 标题里 **exhausted after using AI** 信息密度刚好，把比较对象留给正文展开。

修正后 **Why We Feel More Exhausted After Using AI** 简洁清楚，像朋友在吐槽："明明用了 AI，怎么反而更累？"

## 板块四：5 个真实场景例句

> 每句带语气词 + 连读/弱读提示。下划线 `_xxx_` 表示连读或弱读区域，方便跟读。

### 例句 1（评价 AI 的双面性）

> **Honestly?** AI is _a double-edged sword_ (a double-edged sword) — it speeds things up, but it also raises the bar.

**连读提示**：double-edged 中 /b/ 与 /e/ 衔接；raises the 中 the 弱读

### 例句 2（吐槽改稿一小时）

> **Dude...** I spent an hour editing an email the AI wrote in ten seconds. That's _more work than it's worth_ (more work than it's worth).

**连读提示**：spent an 中 /t/ 与 /æ/ 连读；than it's 中 than 弱读

### 例句 3（描述反复质疑）

> **I mean...** Instead of saving time, I _end up_ (end up) second-guessing every single sentence it writes.

**连读提示**：end up 连读 /endʌp/；second-guessing 中 /d/ 弱读

### 例句 4（吐槽沉迷调提示词）

**连读提示**：rabbit hole 中 /t/ 闪音化；never actually 中 /r/ 连读

**连读提示**：keep it 中 /p/ 与 /ɪ/ 连读；not for 中 for 弱读 /fər/

## 板块五：完整口语表达模板（约 5 分钟 / 650 词）

**口语主题**：Why We Feel More Exhausted After Using AI

**完整口语文稿**：

Hey, what's up everyone. So today I wanna talk about something that's been bugging me for a while — and I bet a lot of you have noticed it too. We keep hearing that AI is supposed to make our lives easier. It saves time, it does the boring work, it's the greatest thing since sliced bread. And yet... I don't know about you, but some days I feel **more exhausted** after using AI than I did working without it. And I'm pretty sure I'm not alone.

It's weird, right? It's a total **paradox**. The tool that's supposed to give us more free time ends up eating more of our energy. So what's going on? Let me break it down.

First, there's the **polish trap**. This is the big one for me. I ask AI to write an email — takes ten seconds. Great. But then I read it, and it's... almost right. The tone is slightly off. There's a weird phrase. The ending feels too formal. So I start editing. And editing. And before I know it, I've spent an hour fixing an email that would've taken me fifteen minutes to write from scratch. The **output** is a starting point, but I **end up** doing more work, not less.

Second, **expectations** went through the roof. Before AI, nobody expected a perfect draft in two seconds. Now? We do. And not just from AI — from ourselves too. AI **raises the bar** for everyone. If the machine can generate something decent in seconds, why is my own work taking hours? So I **second-guess** everything I write. Is this good enough? Could AI do it better? That constant **comparison**? It's mentally draining.

Third — and this is the sneaky one — **decision fatigue**. AI gives you options. Ten versions of a headline, five outlines, three different tones. Sounds great in theory. In practice? Every choice is a tiny decision, and decisions are exhausting. I used to just write my thing and move on. Now I spend energy picking between options that are all fine. It's like being at a restaurant with a hundred dishes on the menu — you leave tired even though you ate well.

And then there's the **rabbit hole** problem. One small task turns into forty minutes of tweaking **prompts**. "Make it more casual." "No, more professional." "Add a joke." "Actually, remove the joke." You fall down the hole, and the original task — the one you actually needed to do — is still sitting there unfinished. The **distractions** are built right into the tool.

So what do we do? I've been experimenting, and here's what actually helps me. First, **keep it simple**. I use AI for one specific thing: drafts. Not final copies. I treat its output like a rough first version, and I give myself permission to rewrite big chunks instead of polishing every word. Second, I set a time limit. Ten minutes of editing, max. When the timer goes off, I ship it. Done is better than perfect. **Quality over quantity**, and honestly? Finished over flawless.

Third, I **take a step back** and remind myself what AI is actually for. It's a tool, not a standard. It's not the boss of me. The point isn't to make everything as good as the machine — the point is to get my life back. And sometimes that means NOT using AI at all. For quick, personal emails? I just write them myself. Faster, more human, zero **anxiety**.

Look, I'm not saying AI is bad. It's genuinely amazing. It's **a double-edged sword** — sharp on both sides. It can save you a ton of time, or it can quietly eat your whole afternoon while you polish, compare, and overthink. The difference is how you use it.

So here's my challenge for you: next time you use AI, notice how you feel afterward. If you're more **exhausted** than before — it's not you, it's the workflow. Change the workflow. Use it for drafts, set a time limit, and **keep it simple**. Your energy is worth more than a perfect email.

Alright, that's my two cents. Go easy on yourself this week, and give yourself some **rest**. Thanks for listening. Catch you next time.
